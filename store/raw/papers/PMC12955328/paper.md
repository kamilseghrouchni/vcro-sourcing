# Baseline plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 as a sensitive marker for the severity of Alzheimer’s disease continuum

## Abstract

### Background

Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 has shown promising diagnostic accuracy for Alzheimer’s disease (AD), but its prognostic utility for AD risk and clinical phenotype remains unclear.

### Methods

We analyzed 580 participants from the Alzheimer’s Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI). Diagnostic performance of p-tau217/Aβ42 for cognitively normal (CN) vs. AD was evaluated. Cox regression and linear mixed-effects models were used to investigate the relationships of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 with AD risk, cognitive decline and brain atrophy. We also quantified the incremental predictive value of p-tau217/Aβ42 for AD risk, cognitive decline and brain atrophy beyond demographic data and the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE). Mediation analyses were conducted to test whether brain structure accounted for associations between p-tau217/Aβ42 and cognition.

### Results

Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 levels were elevated in individuals with amyloid and tau pathology. Combining plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 with MMSE discriminated AD from CN individuals (AUC = 0.988). Higher p-tau217/Aβ42 predicted faster cognitive decline and greater hippocampal atrophy (P < 0.001), increased explained variance for cognitive decline and brain atrophy (ΔmR² = 0.016–0.121). Hippocampal volumes partially mediated the relationships of p-tau217/Aβ42 with cognition (5.3–14.8%). In non-demented participants, the high-risk group of p-tau217/Aβ42 showed higher AD risk (HR = 7.45, 95% CI 4.36–12.74) and faster cognitive decline, remaining significant among those with preserved baseline cognition (MMSE > 28). The addition of p-tau217/Aβ42 improved AD risk prediction beyond MMSE and covariates (ΔC = 0.040), increased time-dependent AUC at 2 years (ΔAUC = 0.030), reduced prediction error (ΔBrier = − 0.017), improved reclassification (NRI/IDI; all P < 0.05).

### Conclusion

Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 is a strong prognostic biomarker for AD progression, adding value beyond demographics and brief cognitive screening. It supports a practical blood-first approach for risk stratification and prioritizing confirmatory testing, with additional utility for diagnostic support and trial enrichment.

## Background

Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a neurodegenerative disorder marked by cognitive decline, amyloid-beta plaques (Aβ), and tau tangles, with an estimated 139 million affected globally by 2050 [1, 2]. Aducanumab and lecanemab have recently received FDA approval for early symptomatic AD, while donanemab met primary and secondary endpoints in phase 3 trials [3–5]. These Aβ-targeting disease-modifying therapies show greater benefits in the early stages of AD [4, 6]. The latest therapeutic developments in AD highlight the essential requirement for sensitive biomarkers to pinpoint candidates for early treatment [7, 8].

In the absence of post-mortem neuropathological confirmation, diagnosis relies on CSF and PET biomarkers, but their cost, limited availability, and invasiveness constrain early-stage use [9, 10]. Blood-based biomarkers offer a minimally invasive, convenient, and cost-effective approach for the early detection and monitoring of the disease [11]. Indeed, alterations in blood concentrations of p-tau181, p-tau217, and p-tau231 have been observed in preclinical AD, allowing differentiation from cognitively unimpaired individuals with non-Alzheimer’s pathology [11, 12]. Among these markers, p-tau217, particularly the combination biomarker p-tau217/Aβ42 ratio, exhibits high diagnostic accuracy and disease specificity [12–15]. Early research efforts primarily focused on the detection of amyloid peptides [11, 16], with comparatively less attention given to the association between p-tau217/Aβ42 and AD risk and cognitive decline [17, 18]. Only fewer studies have investigated the association between plasma p-tau217 levels and cognitive function [17–20], with cognitive assessments restricted to the modified Preclinical Alzheimer Cognitive Composite (mPACC) and MMSE scores. Further studies are needed to clarify the utility of this biomarker for monitoring clinical progression in symptomatic individuals along the AD continuum.

The FDA recently approved the plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 ratio as the first blood-based AD biomarker, but additional validation is needed to confirm its suitability for standard clinical practice. While p-tau217/Aβ42 shows significant potential for early diagnosis and prognosis of AD, they do not consistently align with the clinical presentation [21]. Some people with significant biomarker signs of AD pathology might show mild clinical symptoms, while others with fewer biomarker issues might have more severe cognitive problems [21, 22]. Traditional AD screening methods, such as the MMSE and demographic assessments, may fail to identify high-risk individuals who exhibit underlying pathological progression despite normal cognitive screening results. Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42, as a biomarker, provides detailed pathological information and, when combined with basic clinical data, enhances AD diagnostic performance [11, 23]. However, its independent contribution to AD risk and clinical prognosis remains uncertain. The challenge lies in effectively integrating it into clinical pathways, moving beyond traditional brief scales (e.g., MMSE) to deliver more precise predictions and actionable insights, thus supporting clinical decision-making.

In the present study, to verify the previous findings, we at first described the distribution of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 in the AD continuum, and examined the diagnostic accuracy and its combination with clinical measures for AD. Furthermore, we would rather focus on providing additional evidence on the long-term prognostic relevance of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42, underlining its potential in risk stratification and guiding early intervention strategies. To this end, we have designed the following research contents: (1) explored the cross-sectional and longitudinal associations between p-tau217/Aβ42 and cognition function and brain structure; (2) performed the incremental value of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 in predicting cognitive decline and brain atrophy beyond demographic characteristics and MMSE; (3) investigated the roles of neurodegeneration in mediating the relationship between plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 and cognitive function; (4) determined the risk stratification utility of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 in predicting progression to AD among non-demented individuals; and (5) assessed effect modification by baseline cognitive status, defined by MMSE strata and diagnostic stage, on the association of p-tau217/Aβ42 with AD risk, cognitive decline, and brain atrophy.

## Methods and materials

### Participants

ADNI is an established multi-center cohort in North America. It is launched to test clinical, imaging, genetic, and biochemical biomarkers of AD [24]. The participants, aged 55 to 90 years, were enrolled after approval from the institutional review boards of all participating centers, with written informed consent obtained from all participants or their authorized representatives in accordance with the 1975 Declaration of Helsinki. Detailed information can be found at http://www.adniinfo.org.

We excluded participants who had major neuropsychiatric disorders at baseline or incomplete covariate data or plasma p-tau217/Aβ42. A total of 580 participants were included in the initial analysis at baseline, comprising 315 cognitively normal individuals (CN) (155 with subjective cognitive decline, SCD), 193 with mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and 72 with AD. Any participant with a missing value for variables of interest was excluded in all subsequent association analyses.

### Measurements of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42

The details of plasma p-tau217 in ADNI have been published previously [25, 26]. Blood samples were collected in EDTA tubes, processed to obtain plasma, and transferred to a transfer tube as outlined in the ADNI4 Procedures Manual (V2.0). The plasma aliquots (0.5 mL) were stored at -80 °C until analysis. On the analysis day, each thawed sample (30 min at room temperature) was tested for p-tau 217, Aβ42, and Aβ40 using immunoassays from Fujirebio on the Lumipulse G1200 platform. Samples were analyzed in singlicate and quantified using Fujirebio chemiluminescence immunoassay reagents for Aβ42, Aβ40 and p-tau217 [25]. The plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 ratio is calculated as the plasma concentration of p-tau217 divided by the plasma concentration of Aβ42.

### Measurements of AD CSF biomarkers

Data on CSF biomarkers were accessible in ADNI cohort. The detailed protocols for CSF collection and processing have been previously documented [27]. Briefly, concentrations of CSF Aβ1–42, p-tau181, and total tau proteins (pg/mL) were measured using electrochemiluminescence immunoassays (Elecsys; Roche Diagnostics) on fully automated Elecsys cobas e 601 instruments, as outlined in the UPENNBIOMK_ROCHE_ELECSYS.csv file [28]. Cutoffs of 980 pg/mL or less and 24 pg/mL or greater were used for Aβ42 (UPENN CSF Biomarkers Roche Elecsys Methods; available as a PDF from https://ida.loni.usc.edu/explore/jsp/search_v2/search.jsp?project=ADNI) and p-tau [29], respectively. Participants with Aβ42 levels below the cutoff were classified as A+ (amyloid-positive), and those with p-tau levels above the cutoff were classified as T+ (tau-positive).

### Cognitive measurements

Cognitive function was evaluated using multiple standardized scales. Global cognition was assessed using the cognitive subscale of the Alzheimer’s Disease Assessment Scale – Cognition (ADAS) and MMSE, while specific memory (MEM) and executive functions (EF) were measured through designated neuropsychological tests. The ADAS total score, which comprises 13 tasks across cognitive domains reflects overall cognitive performance, with higher scores indicating more severe impairment [30]. The MMSE is a 30-point scale comprising several cognitive domains [31]. MEM and EF were assessed using composite scores, designed to account for variability across different sites in the administration of neuropsychological tests [32, 33].

### MRI measurements

Structural brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was conducted using either a Siemens Trio 3.0 T or Vision 1.5 T imaging system. Image processing was carried out using Freesurfer software (versions 4.3 and 5.1) to estimate regional brain volumes based on the acquired MRI data [34]. Our analyses focused on specific regions relevant to AD, including the hippocampus, middle temporal lobe, entorhinal cortex, and ventricles. Detailed information regarding MRI acquisition and image processing can be found in previous studies [35].

### Diagnosis of AD dementia

In ADNI, neuropsychological testing and diagnostic criteria details can be found on the ADNI website (http://adni.loni.usc.edu/methods). In brief, AD patients met the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke and the Alzheimer’s Disease and Related Disorders Association (NINCDS-ADRDA) criteria for probable AD [36]. MCI was defined by Petersen criteria [37]. Cognitive diagnoses were recorded annually, with progressors identified by changes from CN to AD dementia or MCI to AD dementia.

### Covariate measurements

Covariates comprised age, gender, years of education, APOE ε4 status, cognitive diagnosis. Demographic characteristics were primarily obtained through participants’ self-report using standardized questionnaires and interviews during screening visits. The data are available at: https://adni.loni.usc.edu. APOE ε2, ε3 and ε4 genotypes were determined by the rs7412 and rs429358 polymorphisms [38].

### Statistical analysis

R software version 4.5.1 was used for statistical analyses. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered as statistical significance. Baseline characteristics for the overall population and subgroups defined by diagnostic category and plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 level were summarized as mean (standard deviation [SD]) for normally distributed continuous variables, median (interquartile range [IQR]) for non-normally distributed variables, and number (percentage) for categorical variables. Between-group comparisons were conducted using the t-test or one-way ANOVA for normally distributed continuous variables, the Mann–Whitney U test or Kruskal–Wallis test for non-normally distributed continuous variables, and the chi-square test for categorical variables.

Differences in plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 levels across demographic and pathology groups were evaluated using boxplots. Non-normally distributed variables were Box-Cox transformed and standardized (z-scores). Group comparisons were conducted with independent t-tests or one-way ANOVA followed by Fisher’s LSD post hoc tests.

The diagnostic performance of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 for distinguishing CN from AD was evaluated using ROC analysis, with the optimal cutoff determined by the Youden index. Cutoff robustness was assessed using resampling and sensitivity analyses across APOE ε4 status, sex, and age strata. This cutoff was used to define biomarker positivity for downstream prognostic analyses and workflow illustration and was distinct from model-based decision thresholds. SCD participants were classified as CN for this analysis. Additional sensitivity analyses evaluated diagnostic performance of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 alone and in combination with clinical variables (age, sex, education, APOE ε4 status) and MMSE using logistic regression–derived ROC curves. AUCs were compared using DeLong’s test with Holm adjustment. Analyses were performed using the “pROC” and “OptimalCutpoints” packages.

Multiple linear regression assessed associations of p-tau217/Aβ42 with cognition and brain structure. Longitudinal associations with cognitive decline and brain atrophy were examined using linear mixed-effects models with random intercepts and time slopes (unstructured covariance), including a time × outcome interaction. Model diagnostics were performed; outliers were removed to meet normality and homoscedasticity assumptions. Skewed outcomes were Box–Cox transformed and standardized as z-scores.

The incremental value of p-tau217/Aβ42 was assessed using LME models for cognitive decline and brain atrophy in non-demented individuals. Hierarchical regression was conducted by first developing a model with sociodemographic characteristics, baseline MMSE scores, and follow-up duration, then adding p-tau217/Aβ42. The increase in explained variance between models was quantified by Nagelkerke’s R² change (ΔR²), reflecting the added predictive value of p-tau217/Aβ42 [39].

Mediation analyses were conducted to quantify the extent to which brain structure could statistically account for the observed association between p-tau217/Aβ42 and cognitive function, using the “mediation” package. Conceptually, we specified p-tau217/Aβ42 as the exposure (X), brain structural measures as mediators (M), and cognitive scores as outcomes (Y). We fit a mediator model (M ~ X + covariates) and an outcome model (Y ~ X + M + covariates) to estimate the indirect (X→M→Y) component of the observed association. The analyses were done using nonparametric bootstrapping with 10,000 iterations to estimate direct effects (DE), indirect effects (IE), the proportion mediated, and P-values. Because the data are observational, mediation estimates rely on assumptions such as no unmeasured confounding, correct model specification, and an assumed temporal ordering; violations may bias estimates. Thus, results should be interpreted cautiously and are reported as a decomposition of observed associations rather than as definitive causal effects.

Multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression and LME models were used to explore the relationship of different p-tau217/Aβ42 risk groups with the risk of AD, cognitive decline and brain atrophy in the non-demented population. The proportional hazards assumption was assessed using Schoenfeld residuals. The interaction and stratified analyses by MMSE (> 28 vs. ≤28) and diagnostic group were performed to assess early-stage utility and clinical heterogeneity in the associations of p-tau217/Aβ42 with incident AD risk, cognitive decline, and brain atrophy. Longitudinal associations were examined using linear mixed-effects models including p-tau217/Aβ42, MMSE/diagnosis, time, and their interaction terms, with the three-way interaction tested by likelihood ratio comparison. Detailed methodology has been described previously [40].

A sensitivity analysis was conducted to assess whether adding plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 provides incremental prognostic information beyond brief cognitive testing for predicting incident AD. Using Cox proportional hazards models, we compared a base model including MMSE and relevant clinical covariates with an augmented model additionally incorporating p-tau217/Aβ42. Model performance and risk stratification were evaluated at multiple follow-up time points (2-year / 4-year follow-up), and the potential clinical utility of the biomarker was illustrated using predefined predicted-risk thresholds of 10% and 20%.

Above all association analyses were adjusted for age, sex, APOE ε4 status, educational attainment, and cognitive diagnosis. Additionally, the intracranial volume (ICV) was further adjusted when the outcome variables were brain volumes.

## Results

### Basic participant characteristics

Participants were stratified into high-risk group (n = 187) and low-risk group (n = 393) using the optimal p-tau217/Aβ42 cutoff. The median age was 72.25 years, and 51.38% were male. APOE ε4 carriers accounted for 42.5% of the cohort. Compared with the low-risk group, the high-risk group was older (P < 0.0001), more often male (P = 0.01), and had a higher APOE ε4 carrier frequency (P < 0.0001). Cognitive impairment was more common in the high-risk group, with higher proportions of AD and MCI (P < 0.0001). The groups also differed in cognitive scores, brain structure, and CSF biomarkers (all P < 0.0001). Education level did not differ between groups (P = 0.08). Detailed characteristics are shown in Table 1. The median follow-up was approximately 2–3 years and the maximum follow-up reached 10 years, with detailed follow-up duration and visit frequency across diagnostic groups provided in the e-Tables 1, 2 and 3. Cognitive performance worsened and MRI atrophy increased across CN/SCD/MCI/AD groups (all P < 0.001), accompanied by higher plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 and greater CSF pathology burden (see e-Table 4).

### Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 across the AD continuum

p-tau217/Aβ42 levels were significantly higher in individuals aged ≥ 65 (P < 0.05, Fig. 1A), males (P < 0.001, Fig. 1B), and APOE ε4 carriers (P < 0.001, Fig. 1C). Concentrations of p-tau217/Aβ42 progressively increased across cognitive diagnostic categories from CN to AD (all P < 0.001, Fig. 1D). It was higher in the A+ (P < 0.001, Fig. 1E) and T+ (P < 0.001, Fig. 1F) groups compared to A − and T− individuals. The concentrations followed the gradient A + T + > A + T− > A − T + > A − T−, with multiple significant pairwise differences (P < 0.001, Fig. 1G). In the combined cognitive diagnosis with AT groups, the highest concentrations were observed in AD A+ participants, followed by MCI A + and CN A+ participants, whereas all A− subgroups within each cognitive category had lower levels (P < 0.001, Fig. 1H).

### Diagnostic performance of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 and in combination with clinical measures and MMSE for AD

Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 differentiated AD from CN with an AUC of 0.895 (95% CI: 0.852–0.938; e-Figure 1). Using the ROC-derived cutoff of 0.0116, sensitivity and specificity were 0.847 and 0.857, respectively, with a Youden index of 0.704 and an accuracy of 0.855 (PPV 0.575; NPV 0.961) (e-Table 5). In resampling analyses, the mean cutoff was 0.012 (SE = 0.002), with mean sensitivity 0.852 and mean specificity 0.868 (e-Table 6). In subgroup analyses, AUCs ranged from 0.838 to 0.931, and subgroup-specific cutoffs ranged from 0.0064 to 0.0136. The overall-cohort cutoff (0.0116) lay within this range and was close to the subgroup estimates for APOE ε4 carriers, females, and individuals aged < 75 years (e-Table 7). The addition of MMSE scores to plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 resulted in a substantial improvement in accuracy, with an AUC of 0.988 (95% CI: 0.974–0.999, P < 0.001), sensitivity reaching 0.986, and specificity at 0.939 (P = 0.839; e-Table 5, e-Table 8).

### The association between plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 and cognition and brain structure

At baseline, higher p-tau217/Aβ42 was associated with poorer cognition (all P ≤ 0.005, Fig. 2A–D) and smaller brain volumes (lower hippocampal, entorhinal, and mid-temporal volumes) as well as larger ventricular volume (all P ≤ 0.015; Fig. 2E–H). Significant interaction effects between p-tau217/Aβ42 and diagnosis were observed for all cognition and some brain structures (hippocampus and mid-temporal volumes) (P < 0.05, e-Table 9, e-Figure 2). Longitudinal analyses showed that baseline p-tau217/Aβ42 was associated with accelerated cognitive decline, as reflected by ADAS (β = 0.07, P < 0.001), MMSE (β = -0.06, P < 0.001), MEM (β = -0.10, P < 0.001), and EF (β = -0.06, P < 0.001) scores (Fig. 3A, e-Table 10). Furthermore, baseline p-tau217/Aβ42 exhibited faster rates of brain atrophy, including the hippocampus (β = -0.03, P < 0.001), mid-temporal cortex (β = -0.05, P < 0.001), and ventricles (β = 0.03, P < 0.001) (Fig. 3B, e-Table 10). Likelihood ratio tests showed that the baseline p-tau217/Aβ42 × diagnosis × time interaction explained significant variance in cognition (ADAS, P = 0.037; MEM, P < 0.001; EF, P = 0.001) and brain structure (hippocampus, P = 0.039; entorhinal cortex, P = 0.021; mid-temporal cortex, P = 0.005) (e-Table 10). Stratified analyses showed increasing β estimates with worsening diagnosis, suggesting faster cognitive decline and brain atrophy with greater cognitive impairment (Fig. 3A–B).

### Incremental value of the plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 levels for prediction of cognitive decline and brain atrophy in non-demented individuals

Model A, which included demographic variables, time, and MMSE as covariates, explained a modest amount of variance for cognitive decline and brain volume measures, with a relatively lower R² value. The addition of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 to Model B led to significant increases in explained variance for all cognitive outcomes, including ADAS, MMSE, MEM, and EF (ΔmR² = 0.037 ~ 0.121, P < 0.001) (Table 2). Meanwhile, hippocampal volume, entorhinal volume, mid-temporal volume, and ventricular volume all exhibited substantial increases in variance explanation upon the addition of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 (ΔmR² = 0.016 ~ 0.045, P < 0.05) (Table 2).

### Sensitivity analysis

In the non-demented cohort, adding p-tau217/Aβ42 group to a Cox model with MMSE and clinical covariates improved AD risk prediction (ΔC = 0.040, 95% CI 0.018–0.068). At 2 years, discrimination increased (ΔAUC = 0.030, P = 0.048) and prediction error decreased (ΔBrier = − 0.017, P = 0.002), with supportive reclassification gains (NRI 1.189 at 2 years and 0.905 at 4 years; IDI 0.173 at 2 years and 0.197 at 4 years; all P < 0.05) (e-Table 15, and 16). Decision curve analysis showed higher net benefit and acceptable calibration (e-Figure 4). To illustrate clinical actionability, applying predicted-risk decision thresholds (10% and 20%) demonstrated more efficient triage with the augmented model. At the 2-year 20% threshold, PPV increased from 0.476 to 0.645 with a lower referral rate (0.194 to 0.161) and fewer confirmatory tests per detected converter (NNT 2.10 to 1.55). Similar patterns were observed at the 2-year 10% threshold (PPV 0.364 to 0.532; NNT 2.75 to 1.88). At 4 years, improvements were most consistent at the 20% threshold (e-Table 17).

## Discussion

In this study, we (1) observed a stepwise distribution of plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 across cognitive states and AD stages, and demonstrated excellent diagnostic performance in combination with the MMSE; (2) demonstrated that risk stratification based on p-tau217/Aβ42 predicts longitudinal cognitive and brain structural decline as well as AD risk, with independent predictive value beyond traditional demographic factors and the MMSE, including among participants with preserved baseline cognition (MMSE > 28); and (3) identified the hippocampus, entorhinal cortex, and middle temporal gyrus as key brain regions in which tau pathology is associated with cognitive performance. Collectively, these findings indicate that plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 is a robust prognostic marker for AD progression, cognitive decline, and brain atrophy. It provides information beyond demographics and MMSE, including in individuals who appear cognitively intact at baseline. Clinically, integrating this blood biomarker into primary care and memory-clinic workflows may improve referral efficiency and resource allocation: biomarker-positive individuals can be prioritized for confirmatory PET/CSF, closer follow-up, and prevention-focused counseling, whereas low-risk individuals can be monitored less intensively at longer intervals.

Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 increased stepwise across the AD continuum, was consistently higher in A+ (especially A+/T+) groups than A−, supporting its sensitivity for early detection and risk stratification [41, 42]. In addition, we confirmed that plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 discriminates AD well, with additional gains when combined with MMSE. However, this should be interpreted cautiously because MMSE alone provides substantial diagnostic separation. Future studies should test whether p-tau217/Aβ42 adds incremental value beyond routine clinical measures, particularly for detecting early pathology and differentiating earlier disease stages. Beyond cross-sectional performance, p-tau217/Aβ42 also showed prognostic utility: an ROC-derived cutoff identified high-risk, non-demented individuals who subsequently had higher AD risk and faster longitudinal neurodegeneration, even with preserved baseline cognition. This aligns with longitudinal evidence linking plasma p-tau217 to early clinical progression and showing predictive performance comparable to medial temporal tau-PET in cognitively unimpaired cohorts [43, 44]. Overall, growing data indicate that combined amyloid–tau blood signatures outperform single markers for tracking early trajectories [45, 46], supporting p-tau217/Aβ42 as a scalable indicator of future cognitive and neurodegenerative decline.

Notably, prior blood-biomarker studies have often reported prognostic associations without clearly demonstrating incremental value beyond readily available clinical information—particularly baseline cognition. Here, we address this gap by testing whether p-tau217/Aβ42 improves risk prediction over brief cognitive assessment and standard clinical covariates, and by translating any gains into decision-relevant measures that reflect downstream workflow impact. In models anchored on MMSE and clinical covariates, adding p-tau217/Aβ42 improved discrimination for incident AD (C-index 0.840 to 0.880) and reduced 2-year prediction error (Brier 0.0687 to 0.0515). Importantly, these improvements were clinically consequential: at a 2-year risk threshold of 20%, the biomarker-augmented model increased PPV (0.476 to 0.645) while reducing the referral rate (0.194 to 0.161). In practical terms, per 100 evaluated patients, referrals would decrease from ~ 19 to ~ 16, while expected true positives among those referred would increase from ~ 9 to ~ 10.

Together, these findings support a pragmatic “blood-first” triage strategy, with implementation tailored to setting. In primary care, p-tau217/Aβ42 could be used to triage symptomatic, non-demented patients—prioritizing expedited memory-clinic referral for those at higher near-term risk while supporting structured follow-up when risk is low. In memory-clinic workflows, where PET/CSF capacity is often constrained, the biomarker could help prioritize confirmatory testing among patients with intermediate pretest probability, while PET/CSF would remain essential when etiologic certainty or treatment eligibility requires confirmation. The incremental value beyond MMSE may be greatest in earlier stages where brief cognitive testing shows ceiling effects or limited specificity (e.g., high MMSE scores, subjective cognitive decline, or subtle impairment), and a pathology-linked blood signature can refine near- to mid-term risk and guide follow-up intensity. Overall, this metric shows promise as an early-warning indicator in the prodromal stages of AD. Clinically, it could support triage and follow-up decisions and facilitate preliminary prescreening for clinical trial enrolment. It is positioned as a risk-stratification tool to anticipate longitudinal progression and guide the need for further diagnostic evaluation.

A single cutpoint may not be ideal for real-world use due to the asymmetric clinical consequences of false negatives and positives, and the inherent ambiguity of intermediate biomarker values. Accordingly, recent work on plasma p-tau217 implementation has emphasized two-cutoff and gray-zone strategies, which may reduce harm from misclassification [47]. In these frameworks, a lower threshold prioritizes sensitivity/NPV for ruling out near-term progression or AD risk, whereas an upper threshold prioritizes specificity/PPV for ruling in elevated risk. Values between them define an indeterminate zone where confirmatory CSF/PET testing is recommended to adjudicate underlying pathology and refine prognosis. This strategy reduces costly or invasive testing with minimal loss in overall classification performance, while highlighting real-world false positives from external threshold shifts [48]. Ratio-based biomarkers may be particularly well suited to a two-cutoff framework by narrowing the indeterminate zone and improving triage efficiency [49]. Notably, our threshold was derived from a cross-sectional ROC analysis and then applied to longitudinal risk and trajectory outcomes, representing a pragmatic “borrowed-cutoff” approach. Alternative strategies could derive prognostic thresholds directly (e.g., time-dependent ROC, or decision-curve–based net benefit) and then validate them externally. Future work should derive two-cutoff thresholds for p-tau217/Aβ42 and quantify gray-zone proportions, triage efficiency.

Converging evidence indicates that plasma p-tau217 is among the most accurate blood-based markers for AD pathophysiology, frequently outperforming p-tau181, p-tau231, and Aβ42/40 for detecting abnormal amyloid and tau status across cohorts [42, 50]. p-tau231 may become abnormal very early during amyloid accumulation, suggesting particular sensitivity to incipient Aβ changes [11]. Together, these findings suggest that p-tau231 may better capture early amyloid emergence. In contrast, p-tau217 provides the strongest and most stable signal for integrated A/T identification and prognosis, outperforming p-tau231, p-tau181, and Aβ42/40 in tracking longitudinal clinical deterioration and brain atrophy [20]. However, because ADNI blood biomarkers were assayed at different time points in partially overlapping subsets with unequal sample sizes, we could not perform head-to-head comparisons. Future studies should compare p-tau217/Aβ42 with alternative plasma biomarkers within a unified decision-analytic framework, assess site-specific threshold setting under varying prevalence and resource constraints.

Recent studies have shown subicular hippocampal atrophy partly mediating the association between p-tau and cognition [51, 52]. We further observed that additional middle temporal lobe regions may statistically account for part of the association between p-tau217/Aβ42 and cognition. However, the mediated proportions were modest, consistent with atrophy being a downstream, macroscopic marker and only one of several pathways linking tau-related pathology to cognition. In addition, synaptic dysfunction, neuroinflammation, and oxidative stress may also contribute to cognitive impairment, partly independent of brain atrophy. We hypothesize that higher plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 reflects greater tau pathology [53], which may be associated with neuronal dysfunction and regional atrophy [54]. Experimental studies in neurodegenerative models have shown that modulation of tau-related toxicity, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammatory pathways can influence neuronal integrity and cognitive outcomes, supporting their relevance in AD [55]. Concurrently, a higher plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 may reflect extensive amyloid deposition in the brain [15] and these individuals may exhibit significant neuroinflammation [56], further exacerbating cognitive impairment. Cellular stress-response pathways involved in DNA damage signaling are increasingly recognized as regulators of cell survival and aging, suggesting shared responses to chronic cellular injury [57]. Mechanistic investigations in non-neurological ischemic models highlight the central role of oxidative stress and inflammatory cascades in tissue degeneration, reinforcing their relevance to neurodegenerative diseases [58]. Moreover, tau phosphorylation at T217 has been linked to developing post-synaptic sites and synaptic impairment [59], and synapse loss is among the strongest neuropathological correlates of cognitive impairment in AD. Notably, our mediation analyses are observational and do not establish causality. Causal interpretation would require strong assumptions (e.g., no unmeasured confounding and correct temporal ordering). Future animal experiments will be needed to validate the causal mechanisms underlying these potential associations.

There were several limitations. Firstly, baseline plasma biomarkers were measured only once in this study, and short-term fluctuations may have influenced the results; longitudinal assessments could improve predictive precision. Because plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 is subject to within-person variability and assay-related noise, reliance on a single baseline measurement may introduce nondifferential error, attenuating model effect estimates and increasing misclassification. Repeated sampling and trajectory-based modeling may yield more stable prognostic stratification. Secondly, potential confounders such as renal function were not accounted for, which may affect biomarker concentrations. Renal dysfunction and common comorbidities—including systemic inflammation, vascular burden, and multimorbidity—also may shift circulating Aβ and p-tau concentrations [60–62]. Chronic kidney disease–related increases in plasma p-tau217 can be comparable in magnitude to amyloid-related group differences and may influence reference ranges and cutpoint selection [63]. Potential mechanisms include reduced clearance, altered plasma volume or protein binding, and neurovascular injury with blood–brain barrier dysfunction [61, 63]. Incomplete comorbidity ascertainment could introduce residual confounding or effect modification, weaken calibration, and limit cutpoint transportability across populations with different case-mix [61, 64]. Ratio-based measures may partially mitigate renal-function–related confounding [49]. Thirdly, spectrum effects from biological and clinical heterogeneity across CN/SCD/MCI strata and demographic subgroups argue against a one-size-fits-all threshold, favoring subgroup-tailored or externally recalibrated approaches [65]. A cutpoint from an AD-versus-CN ROC may be miscalibrated across strata with varying pretest risks, competing non-AD pathologies, and AD progression rates. Although we adjusted for baseline diagnosis and tested interactions, limited subgroup sizes and event counts constrain inference and may obscure subgroup-specific decision points. Fourth, p-tau217 platforms are not interchangeable. Between-assay differences in absolute concentrations imply assay-specific cutpoints, and the net value of double-cutoff workflows may depend on both platform characteristics and cohort context [47]. Borderline values may warrant confirmatory testing, and externally derived cutoffs with lower specificity raise false-positive concerns [66]. Accordingly, future studies should prioritize external validation across diverse, community-based cohorts and platforms, with prespecified recalibration to ensure stable risk stratification and cutpoint transportability.

## Conclusions

Our study shows that plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 is a sensitive marker across the AD spectrum, with both diagnostic and prognostic value. The ratio predicted AD risk, cognitive decline, and brain atrophy with high accuracy. Plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 adds prognostic information beyond demographic factors and MMSE. Overall, plasma p-tau217/Aβ42 appears to be a practical and accessible tool with significant implications for guiding screening and prediction. Future research should further validate its utility in broader community populations.
